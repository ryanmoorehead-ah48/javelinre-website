When a property management organization is struggling, the first instinct is to redraw the org chart. New titles, new reporting lines, maybe a new department. In my experience the chart is rarely the problem. The problem lives in the spaces between the boxes: the hand-off from leasing to compliance, from maintenance to leasing, from the site to accounting.

At Parkchester and later at GRC Management, the biggest gains didn't come from reorganizing people. They came from deciding, for every recurring task, who owns it at each moment and what "done" means before it moves to the next person.

## Three questions for every process

**Who owns it right now?** Not which department. Which person. If the answer is "the team," nobody owns it.

**What does done look like?** A unit is not "ready" when the painter leaves. It's ready when leasing can show it and compliance can certify a household for it.

**How does the next person know?** A hand-off that depends on someone remembering to send an email will fail the week that person is out.

> Structure follows accountability. Get the hand-offs right and the org chart mostly draws itself.

## Where to start

Pick the one process that costs the most money when it breaks. For most residential portfolios that's the vacancy pipeline or recertifications. Map it on one page, name an owner for every step, and review it weekly for a month. You'll learn more about your organization in those four meetings than in any restructuring plan.
