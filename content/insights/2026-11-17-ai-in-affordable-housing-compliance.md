Affordable housing runs on paperwork. Every move-in and every annual recertification means collecting income documents, verifying them, calculating eligibility and assembling a file that will hold up to an audit. Most of that work is reading, checking and chasing, which is exactly where AI is now useful.

## Where AI helps today

**Document intake.** Reading pay stubs, benefit letters and bank statements, extracting the figures and flagging what's missing before a file reaches a specialist.

**Chasing.** Following up with applicants for missing documents, in plain language and at the hours applicants are actually available.

**Consistency.** Applying the same checklist to every file, so the quality of a recertification doesn't depend on who processed it or how busy the week was.

## Where people still decide

Eligibility determinations, judgment calls on unusual income and every conversation that involves a household's circumstances should stay with trained staff. The goal isn't to remove compliance specialists. It's to give each of them back the hours they spend on data entry so they can handle more households, more accurately.

> The measure of AI in housing isn't how impressive the demo is. It's whether files are cleaner and units are leased faster.

That's the problem we set out to solve at Arlo, and it's the standard I'd hold any tool to: fewer errors, faster cycle times and a file that's ready for the auditor the day it's completed.
