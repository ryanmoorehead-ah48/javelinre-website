Why East Africa belongs on a real estate investor's map

By Ryan Moorehead · Emerging Markets: East Africa

Most U.S. real estate investors have never looked seriously at East Africa. I think that's starting to change, and the investors who learn the region early, through partnerships rather than one-off deals, will be well positioned.

The fundamentals

Cities like Nairobi are growing quickly, with a young, urbanizing population and a middle class that needs quality housing, retail and workspace. Demand for well-built, well-managed housing outpaces what the formal market delivers. That gap is where disciplined developers and operators can create value, and where operating know-how from markets like New York translates directly.

What it takes to enter

Local partners first. Land, permitting, construction and leasing all depend on relationships. The right local developer or operator is worth more than any market report.

Operations as the edge. Property management, maintenance and tenant services are often where projects fall short. Investors who bring operating discipline protect their returns.

Patience and structure. Currency, title and financing work differently. Good deals are structured for those realities from the start.

The opportunity isn't just capital looking for yield. It's operating experience looking for markets that need it.

My own work in the region, from building partnerships in Kenya to distributing Afrobica coffee in Uganda, has convinced me that this is a long-term opportunity worth understanding now. In future posts I'll share what I'm learning on the ground.
